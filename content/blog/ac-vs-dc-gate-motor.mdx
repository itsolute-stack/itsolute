Every gate motor quote comes down to a choice between two tiers. Most people are given the price difference and left to guess what it buys.

Here is what actually separates them, and when each is the right answer.

## The price gap

Our starting prices for a sliding gate, both including GST and covering the motor kit:

| Region | Standard (AC) | Premium (DC) | Gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kerala | ₹34,000 | ₹39,500 | ₹5,500 |
| Karnataka | ₹39,000 | ₹44,500 | ₹5,500 |

Swing and roller gates are DC throughout — the choice only arises on sliding gates, which is where most people meet it.

## What the money actually buys

### Starting and stopping

An **AC motor** engages fairly abruptly. It goes from stopped to moving, and from moving to stopped, without much in between.

A **DC motor** ramps. It accelerates into the travel and slows before the limit.

On a light gate this is a refinement. On a heavy one it is structural: every abrupt start is a shock absorbed by the gate's frame, its hinges or its rollers. Over years, the gate that was eased into motion is in better condition than the one that was snapped into it.

### Noise

Follows directly from the above. A DC motor on a clean track is quiet enough that the gate rolling is the louder component. An AC motor announces itself more.

Whether that matters depends on where the gate is. At a house whose bedroom faces the compound wall, it matters at 6am.

### Battery backup

This is the difference most people have heard of, and it is often stated too simply.

A **DC motor** takes battery backup on two 12V batteries — built in on some brands, a ₹5,500 add-on on others. Self-contained: the gate keeps working whether or not the house has anything else.

An **AC motor** has no battery of its own. It is not helpless in a cut — it runs from your **inverter connection**, which we wire during installation if you ask. But that depends on the house having an inverter with capacity to spare.

Neither is wrong. The DC version is independent; the AC version borrows from the house. If your home already runs on an inverter through every cut, the AC route is perfectly sound and costs you nothing extra.

**Every motor, on both tiers, has a manual release key.** Whatever else fails, the gate opens by hand. That is the part worth knowing before you spend anything on backup.

### Voice control

DC only. Alexa or Google Home, on compatible setups.

It cannot be added to an AC motor later, at any price. So if voice control is something you want, it is not a feature comparison — it is the decision, and everything else follows from it.

## The honest recommendation

**Choose AC when:** the gate is at a house, gets used a handful of times a day, is not unusually heavy, and either you have an inverter or you are content with the manual release during a cut. You are not buying an inferior motor. You are buying one that suits the duty.

**Choose DC when:** the gate is heavy, or it cycles constantly at a shared entrance, or you want voice control, or you want backup that does not depend on the rest of the house, or the gate is somewhere the noise will annoy someone.

The case that most often justifies the upgrade is **duty cycle** rather than any single feature. A gate opening eight times a day will outlive its owner on either tier. A gate opening eighty times a day will show you the difference.

## What this does not change

Worth stating plainly, because quotes sometimes imply otherwise:

- **Safety sensors** are in both tiers. A photocell pair is not a premium feature, and a quote omitting it is not a cheaper version of the same system.
- **Two remotes** come with both.
- **RFID** is an add-on on both, quoted after the survey.
- **Warranty** is the manufacturer's, from two years, and varies by brand rather than by tier.

## The one question to ask a quote

**"Is this AC or DC?"**

A surprising number of quotes do not say. If two numbers differ and neither names the tier, you are not comparing the same thing, and the cheaper one is cheaper for a reason that will become apparent in about eighteen months.
