In the older parts of Bengaluru — JP Nagar, Jayanagar, Basavanagudi and the established pockets of the south and west — the gate on the property is usually decades old. It was built from heavier section than anything a fabricator would quote today, hung on masonry pillars, and it has outlasted several cars.

The instinct is to replace it before automating. In most cases that is the wrong call.

## Why keeping the gate usually wins

Two reasons, and the second matters more than people expect.

**It is cheaper.** A retrofit buys a motor kit and installation. A replacement buys a motor kit, a fabricated gate, and the installation of both.

**The old gate is often better.** Heavier section, deeper welds, and pillars built when nobody was economising on masonry. A replacement is a lighter gate on the same pillars, which is not an upgrade.

What stops a retrofit is never age. It is condition.

## The four checks

These are what we look at, in order, and any of them can change the answer.

### 1. Frame stiffness

An automatic gate is driven from **one point** — the motor pushes or pulls at one end. A frame that flexes under that load transmits the drive badly and wears unevenly.

Older gates are usually stiff enough. Where one is not, the fix is normally a stiffening member welded in, which is minor work and far short of a replacement.

### 2. Hinges, or the track

On a **swing gate**: hinges that have dropped over decades change the arc the arm has to drive. The motor absorbs that difference until it cannot. Hinges are often the single item that needs attention before automation.

On a **sliding gate**: push it by hand, slowly, the whole way. If it drags or catches at a point, a motor will fight that every day. Usually it is a settled track section, a worn roller, or debris in the channel.

### 3. The pillars

This one gets skipped and should not. An arm motor transfers real force into the gate pillar on every cycle. A pillar that has held a gate for forty years has been doing a different job — holding weight, not resisting a push.

Sound masonry is generally fine. A pillar that is cracked, leaning or loose at the base needs attention first, and we will say so rather than bolting a motor to it.

### 4. Ground clearance

If the gate scrapes, something has moved — a hinge, the track, or the ground itself. A motor does not care that it is scraping; it has more force than you do and will keep going. That is how a scrape becomes wear on both the gate and the motor.

The kit covers the motor sized to your gate, 2 remotes, a safety sensor pair, the rack or track for a sliding gate, and welding.

**The retrofit variable is the preparation.** Hinge work, a stiffening member, a track section, pillar repair — whatever your gate needs first. We quote that separately from the automation, deliberately, so you can see the two numbers and decide. A retrofit that needs substantial preparation can tip towards a replacement, and you should be able to see that for yourself rather than being told.

## Double gates

Common in these neighbourhoods, and not a problem. A double-leaf gate takes one arm motor per leaf, synchronised so the two move as one.

Two things follow. It costs more, because it is two motors. And it takes longer to commission, because synchronisation has to be set properly — two motors that are not matched meet badly at the centre, look wrong and wear unevenly. More on that in [single vs double swing gate openers](/blog/single-vs-double-swing-gate-opener).

## What we will tell you honestly

Sometimes the answer is that the gate is not worth automating as it stands. A frame too light to drive, pillars that need rebuilding, a track beyond repair — in those cases we say so, and we say it at the survey rather than after taking a deposit.

That is rarer than you might think. Most gates in the older south of the city are good candidates, and the preparation is minor.

## What happens next

The survey on an older gate is a longer conversation than on a new one, because there is more to look at: the frame, the hinges or track, the pillars, the clearance, and where power can come from. The quote that follows separates preparation from automation.
