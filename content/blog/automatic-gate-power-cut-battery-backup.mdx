In Kerala, "what happens when the power goes?" isn't a hypothetical. It's the question that should decide which motor you buy.

The answer has one fork in it, and everything else follows from that fork.

## It comes down to AC or DC

**An AC motor stops.** No battery backup is possible on an AC motor — not as an upgrade, not for extra money. When the power goes, the gate stays where it is, and you open it by hand with the manual release key.

**A DC motor can keep working.** Battery backup is available: built into the motor on some brands, and a **₹5,500** add-on on others. The control board switches over when the mains drops and the gate opens and closes as normal.

That's the whole decision. AC is the lower starting price and does the job; DC costs more, runs smoother and quieter, can have backup, and is the only one that can take voice control. If outages are frequent where you are, the difference is worth paying. If they aren't, AC is honest value.

What you should not do is assume backup is included. It isn't included on anything — it's either built into the DC motor you chose, or it's an add-on.

## Every motor has a manual release

Whatever you buy, there is a key that disengages the drive so the gate can be pushed by hand.

Three things to insist on:

- **Be shown it at handover**, physically, and try it yourself once.
- **Know where the key lives**, and make sure it isn't only inside the gate you're locked out of. This sounds obvious. It is the single most common lockout we get called about.
- **Make sure household staff or security know it too**, not just you.

On a boom barrier the equivalent matters even more, because emergency vehicles may need the arm raised immediately.

## How the backup works, when you have it

The battery sits with the control board at the motor. In normal operation mains power runs the gate and trickle-charges the battery. When the mains drops, the board switches over.

Two consequences worth knowing:

1. **It is independent of your house supply.** The backup is local to the gate, on its own wiring. It doesn't care whether your inverter is working.

2. **It is always either charging or discharging.** A battery kept in a hot enclosure and cycled regularly ages faster than the datasheet suggests. In Kerala's heat, assume the shorter end of any stated life.

## How many openings do you really get?

Be sceptical of anyone who answers this with a confident single number. It depends on gate weight, battery capacity, battery age and how long the outage runs.

A healthy backup on a typical residential gate covers a normal day's use without drama. The useful way to think about it isn't the day-one number — it's that the number declines, and you won't be told when.

## What to ask a quote for

- **Is this motor AC or DC?** Everything else follows from the answer.
- **If DC: is the battery built in, or is it the ₹5,500 add-on?**
- **Is the battery a replaceable, standard part**, so it can be swapped in a few years without replacing the control board?
- **Where is the manual release, and who will be shown it?**
- **Is a photocell pair included?** It should be — that's the safety sensor, and it comes with every installation we do.

A quote that's noticeably cheaper is often an AC motor being compared against a DC one. That isn't a discount on the same system — it's a different system, and the power-cut behaviour is the difference.

## Do you need an inverter as well?

Generally no. Running a DC gate from the house inverter instead of a dedicated backup is a step down in reliability: you've made the gate dependent on another system, its wiring, and its own battery's health.

And an inverter does not rescue an AC gate motor's backup problem, because the limitation is in the motor, not the supply.

## The maintenance point

Where a backup battery is fitted, it is the one part guaranteed to need replacing. Everything else — motor, board, sensors — should run for years with adjustment. The battery is a consumable, and its decline is invisible until the day it matters.

That's the practical argument for an AMC on a DC gate: the visit checks battery health, sensor alignment, force limits, and the track or hinges, which together are almost everything that causes a gate to fail.

## The bottom line

Decide AC or DC on how much a power cut would actually bother you, because that choice is permanent and backup cannot be retrofitted to AC. Whichever you pick, learn where the manual release key is and don't keep the only copy behind the gate.
