A boom barrier is the right answer when a gate would be too slow. At a complex where two hundred cars pass before ten in the morning, the few seconds a gate takes to slide open become a queue onto the road.

Here is what one costs in Bengaluru, and what actually drives that number.

## The starting price

**From ₹65,000 including GST**, for a DC barrier with an arm up to 5 m.

That covers the barrier, 2 remote controls and a safety sensor pair. Installation is quoted after the free site survey.

## Duty cycle, not lane width

This is the single most important thing to understand before comparing quotes.

Lane width tells you the arm length. It tells you almost nothing about which barrier you need. What wears a barrier out is **how many times it moves**, and that varies enormously between sites that look identical:

- A small office with thirty staff might cycle its barrier sixty times a day.
- A 300-flat complex can cycle one **a thousand times a day**, most of them concentrated into two short windows.

A barrier specified for the first and installed at the second will fail inside a year, and the failure will look like bad luck rather than the specification error it was. When we survey, we ask for vehicle counts and when they pass, not just the lane dimensions.

If a quote you are holding did not ask that question, it is a price rather than a specification.

## What the installation figure depends on

- **The foundation.** A barrier has to be founded properly. On fresh ground that is straightforward; on an existing slab, in a basement, it is a different job.
- **The cable run.** Power at the guard point is common and helpful. Power at the barrier position is not always the same thing.
- **Headroom**, in a basement or podium car park, which has to clear the raised arm.
- **Loop detectors**, where vehicle sensing is wanted in the road surface.
- **Access hardware**, if RFID is going in at the same time.

## Access: remotes, tags and who holds what

Two remotes come with the barrier. For a household that is the end of the conversation. For a shared entrance it is the start of one.

**Remotes** suit a smaller complex where every resident vehicle is known and the list rarely changes.

**RFID tags or cards**, issued per vehicle, suit a larger one — and the reason is administrative rather than technical. When a flat changes hands, a tag can be deactivated. A remote cannot be deactivated; it can only be asked for back, and it often is not.

RFID is an add-on quoted after the survey, because the cost scales with how many credentials you need and how many read points. We do not publish a number for it for the same reason we do not publish an installation figure: it would be a guess.

## Power cuts at a shared entrance

For a home gate, battery backup is a convenience. For a shared entrance it is close to essential, and we treat it that way.

A barrier stuck down blocks every resident at once. A barrier stuck up defeats the point of having one. Backup is two 12V batteries, built in on some brands and a ₹5,500 add-on on others.

There is also a manual release, so the arm can be raised by hand and left up until power returns. We make sure the security team knows how to use it, because the person who needs it at two in the morning is rarely the person who signed the contract.

## Where Bengaluru prices sit

A flat ₹5,000 per product above our Kerala pricing. That is the cost of serving the city from the Bellandur office, named rather than buried in the installation quote.

## Servicing is not optional here

A barrier is the hardest-working thing we install. Nothing else cycles as often, and arm balance, spring tension, loop detectors and the motor under sustained load all drift predictably.

For a shared entrance, a scheduled AMC is a different proposition from one on a house gate: the cost of a failure is shared by everyone who lives there, and the failures that strand a complex are usually the ones a scheduled visit would have caught.

## What to ask a quote

1. **Did anyone ask how many vehicles pass per day?** If not, the motor duty is a guess.
2. **Is a photocell pair included?** An arm coming down on a car is the thing it prevents.
3. **Is backup included or extra?** At a shared entrance this matters more than the headline price.
4. **What does RFID cost for our actual number of flats?**
