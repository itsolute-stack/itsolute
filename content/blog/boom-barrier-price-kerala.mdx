A boom barrier is a different tool from an automatic gate. It isn't there to secure a boundary — it's there to control vehicles, quickly, hundreds of times a day. Price it on that basis and the decisions get clearer.

## Starting price

**From ₹59,000**, excluding GST, installed.

That includes what we fit as standard on every entrance automation job:

- **Safety sensors (photocells)** — so the arm never comes down on a vehicle
- **Battery backup** — operation continues through a power cut
- **Remote and RFID access** — tags or cards for regular users, plus remotes
- **Installation, wiring and configuration**
- **A free site survey** first

An AMC is available separately, and on a barrier it matters more than on a home gate — see duty cycle below.

## Sizing the arm

Measure the clear width of the lane the barrier has to close. That's the span the arm must cover, and it drives the hardware choice:

- A **single car lane** is commonly around 3 metres.
- **Wider commercial entries**, two-lane entries, or anything taking trucks need a longer arm.

Arm length isn't a trivial variable. A longer arm is a longer lever, so it loads the mechanism more and needs a motor specified accordingly. Fitting an over-long arm to an under-specified unit is a reliable way to wear out the gearbox.

One practical note: where the lane is wide but you don't want a very long arm, it's often better to narrow the lane with a kerb or island than to oversize the barrier.

## Duty cycle is the real specification

This is where boom barriers differ most from residential gates, and where cheap quotes come apart.

A home gate might open six times a day. An apartment entry can run 300–600 cycles a day. A commercial yard or a paid parking entrance can run more. Over a year, that's the difference between a few thousand operations and a quarter of a million.

Motors are rated for this. A unit specified for intermittent residential use, installed on an apartment entrance, won't last — and the failure won't be covered by anything, because it was the wrong specification from the start. When you compare barrier quotes, the rated cycles-per-hour figure matters more than the price.

## Choosing the access method

| Users | Usually best |
| --- | --- |
| Residents and staff, regular | RFID cards or tags — one per vehicle, cancellable if lost |
| Small fixed group | Remotes |
| Visitors | Guard button, intercom, or keypad |
| High-volume commercial | RFID plus a logged, managed tag list |

RFID is the default for anything with turnover of users, for one reason: when a tag is lost or a tenant leaves, you cancel that tag. With shared remotes or a shared code, you're re-issuing to everybody.

## What moves the quote

1. **Arm length** and the matching motor specification.
2. **Duty cycle** — how many vehicles per day, which sets the class of unit.
3. **Access method** beyond the standard remote and RFID.
4. **Civil work** — barriers usually need a concrete base, and that may not exist yet.
5. **Cable runs** — distance to power, and whether cable can go in conduit or needs trenching. On a parking entrance this often crosses a driveway, which is real work.
6. **Loop detectors or additional safety** — ground loops to detect a waiting or departing vehicle, where traffic justifies it.

## Where barriers get installed badly

Three recurring problems, all avoidable at the survey stage:

- **No safety sensor, or one aimed badly.** The arm has to detect a vehicle underneath it. Alignment is not optional, and it needs re-checking over time.
- **The barrier sited too close to the road.** A vehicle waiting for the arm then blocks traffic. Set it back far enough for one vehicle to clear the road while waiting.
- **No thought about exit.** An entry barrier with no exit plan creates a queue at the wrong end. Decide early whether one barrier serves both directions or you need two.

## Barrier, gate, or both

If you're weighing this against gate automation, the split is simple: a barrier manages vehicle flow, a gate secures a boundary. A barrier stops cars, not people — it's a traffic device, not a security device.

Plenty of sites want both: the gate closed overnight for the perimeter, the barrier handling vehicles during the day. If the boundary is your actual concern, start with [automatic gate pricing](/blog/automatic-gate-price-kerala) instead.
