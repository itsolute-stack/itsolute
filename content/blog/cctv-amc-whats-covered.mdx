The whole point of CCTV is that the footage is there when you need it. And the cruel thing about CCTV is that it fails in exactly the way that hides this: silently. A camera drops offline, a hard drive wears out, recording stops — and nothing looks wrong until the day something happens and you discover the system stopped recording weeks ago. A CCTV maintenance contract exists to prevent precisely that. Here's what one actually covers, and why "install and forget" is a real risk for homes and businesses alike.

## Why CCTV fails quietly

Unlike a laptop that clearly dies, CCTV can look perfectly fine while not doing its job:

- A **camera goes offline** — the others still record, so nobody notices one is dark.
- The **hard drive fills or wears out** — drives that record 24/7 have a limited life, and when they fail, recording can stop.
- **Storage retention silently shrinks** — you think you have 30 days of footage; you actually have three.
- **Remote viewing breaks** after a router change or app update, and nobody checks until they need it.
- A **camera drifts** out of position or its lens gets dirty, quietly covering the wrong area.

None of these announce themselves. That's the risk an AMC is built to remove.

## What a CCTV AMC covers

A proper CCTV maintenance contract turns "hope it's still recording" into "checked and confirmed." Typically it includes:

- **Camera health checks** — confirming every camera is online, recording, and pointed where it should be.
- **Recording and storage verification** — making sure footage is actually being written and that you're retaining the number of days you expect.
- **Lens cleaning and angle adjustment** — so image quality doesn't quietly degrade.
- **Firmware and software updates** — keeping cameras and the recorder current and secure.
- **Remote viewing checks** — confirming you can still see footage on your phone when it matters.
- **Priority response** — when a camera or recorder does fail, it's fixed quickly, not whenever someone gets around to it.

The exact scope is written into the contract, so there's no ambiguity about what's covered.

## The storage question most people miss

The most common silent failure is storage. Hard drives that record continuously are consumables — they wear out, typically in a few years. When one fails or fills, recording can stop entirely. An AMC watches drive health and retention, and replaces a drive *before* it takes your footage with it. If you set up CCTV a few years ago and have never thought about the drive since, that's the most likely thing quietly waiting to fail. ## Who it's for

CCTV AMC isn't only for big installations. A home with cameras at the gate, a shop with four cameras over the counter, an office with sixteen across the premises — all face the same silent-failure risk, and all benefit from someone checking the system actually works. For businesses, CCTV maintenance can be folded into your [IT AMC](/amc) under one contract; for homes and smaller setups, it can stand alone.

CCTV you never check is CCTV you're trusting on faith. Given that its entire value is being there on the one day you need it, that's a strange thing to leave to chance. A maintenance contract is cheap insurance that the footage will actually be there.
