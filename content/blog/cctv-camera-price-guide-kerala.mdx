CCTV pricing in Kerala ranges enormously, and the cheapest quote is rarely the cheapest system to own. The difference usually isn't the camera on the wall — it's what you can't see: the storage, the warranty, and whether the whole thing actually records reliably.

Here's what CCTV really costs — for a home or a business — and where the money goes.

## What determines the price

Four things drive most of the cost:

1. **The cameras** — resolution and type (HD analog vs IP) and how many you need.
2. **The recorder and storage** — a DVR or NVR plus a hard drive sized for how many days of footage you want to keep.
3. **Installation** — cabling, mounting, and configuration, which depends on your building.
4. **Remote viewing and extras** — mobile app setup, night vision, and any alerts.

A quote that looks cheap has usually economised on one of these — most often storage retention or genuine, warrantied cameras.

## Camera types and rough prices

**HD analog cameras (2MP–5MP):** the budget-friendly workhorse. Good general coverage for a shop or small office. Lower per-camera cost, runs on coaxial cabling to a DVR.

**IP cameras (4MP and up):** sharper footage — enough to read a face or a number plate — with smarter features like motion zones. Higher per-camera cost, runs over network cabling (PoE) to an NVR. Worth it where detail actually matters: entrances, tills, gates.

For most businesses the right answer is a mix — IP cameras where you need detail, HD cameras for general coverage. (We break down the trade-off fully in our guide to [IP vs analog CCTV](/blog/ip-vs-analog-cctv).)

## Recorder and storage

The recorder (DVR for analog, NVR for IP) and its hard drive are where quotes quietly differ. More cameras, higher resolution, and longer retention all need more storage. Most businesses want 15–30 days of footage; a bigger drive costs a little more but is often the difference between having the footage you need and losing it before you check.

## Realistic package prices in Kerala (2026)

Installed, indicative starting points:

- **4-camera HD setup** — gate, entrance, and key-area coverage for a home or small shop. From around **₹14,000–20,000**.
- **8-camera setup** — fuller coverage for a business or office, often with a mix of IP and HD cameras. From around **₹32,000**.
- **16+ camera IP system** — schools, warehouses, larger premises, with extended storage. From around **₹90,000** upward.

Final pricing always follows a site survey — camera count, resolution, cable runs, and retention all move the number.

## What to watch for in a cheap quote

- **Unwarrantied cameras** — grey-market units with no manufacturer warranty fail sooner and can't be replaced cleanly.
- **Undersized storage** — a drive that only holds three or four days of footage is useless if you check a week later.
- **Half-configured remote viewing** — "you can see it on your phone" often means it worked once, on the installer's phone, and was never set up on yours.
- **No dedicated network** — IP cameras dumped onto your business network can slow it down; they belong on their own isolated segment.

## Ongoing costs

The cameras run on your power and network — no subscription. The optional ongoing cost is maintenance: an AMC that checks camera health, recording, and storage on a schedule, plus eventual hard-drive replacement (drives recording 24/7 wear out over a few years). It's cheap insurance against the worst outcome — discovering the system stopped recording weeks before the day you needed it.

## The bottom line

Judge CCTV not on the day-one price but on whether the footage will be there when something happens. That comes down to genuine cameras, enough storage, a dedicated network, and remote viewing that actually works.
