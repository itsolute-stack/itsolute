Ask a small business owner if they have a backup and most say yes. Ask when they last restored from it, and the room goes quiet. Backup is one of those things everyone assumes is handled until the day it isn't — and by then it's too late. This is a plain guide to backing up your business data properly, and to the difference between the options Kerala SMBs actually use.

## Sync is not backup (this trips up almost everyone)

The most common backup mistake is assuming OneDrive or Google Drive already covers you. They're brilliant tools, but they do **file sync**, which is not the same as backup:

- **Sync** mirrors your current files to the cloud. Great for working across devices and surviving a dead laptop — but it mirrors *everything*, including mistakes. Delete a file, or let ransomware encrypt it, and that change syncs to the cloud too.
- **Backup** keeps versioned copies over time, so you can roll back to how things were *before* the deletion or the attack.

Sync is worth having. But if it's your only "backup," you're one bad click away from losing data everywhere at once. You need real backup as well.

## The 3-2-1 rule, in plain terms

The simplest rule that actually keeps data safe:

- **3 copies** of your data
- on **2 different types of media**
- with **1 copy off-site**

For a typical SMB that means: the live copy on your computer or server, a local backup (an external drive or a NAS in the office), and an encrypted cloud backup off-site. If a drive fails, you have the local backup. If the office floods or is burgled, you have the cloud. No single event takes out everything.

## The options Kerala SMBs actually use

**Microsoft 365 / OneDrive or Google Workspace / Drive.** If you're already on one of these — and most SMBs should be, see our [Microsoft 365 vs Google Workspace comparison](/blog/microsoft-365-vs-google-workspace) — you have solid file sync and some version history built in. Treat it as one layer, not the whole plan.

**A dedicated cloud backup service.** Tools like Backblaze or similar do *true* versioned backup — automated, encrypted, off-site, with history you can roll back through. This is the layer most SMBs are missing, and it's the one that saves you from ransomware and mistakes, not just dead hardware.

**A local NAS or external drive.** Fast to restore from and cheap per gigabyte, this is your second media type. On its own it's not enough (a fire or theft takes it with the originals), but as part of 3-2-1 it's valuable.

**Application-specific backups.** Some systems need their own handling — Tally is the classic example, which we cover in detail in our [Tally backup guide](/blog/tally-backup-setup-guide).

## Building a backup you can actually trust

A good SMB backup setup usually combines all of the above:

1. Your working files sync to Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace.
2. A dedicated backup service keeps versioned, encrypted, off-site copies.
3. A local backup gives you fast restores for everyday mistakes.
4. Critical apps like Tally get their own scheduled backups.
5. **Everything is automated**, so it never depends on someone remembering.
6. **Restores are tested**, so you know it works before you need it.

That last point is the one everyone skips and the one that matters most. An untested backup is a guess.
