Most villa gates in Bengaluru are in gated layouts, and most of those layouts were built to a pattern: a plot with a compound wall, a driveway, and a gate as wide as the frontage allows. That pattern makes the automation decision fairly predictable — until the ground is not level, which in parts of this city it frequently is not.

Here is how the decision actually goes.

## The layout makes sliding the default

A sliding gate needs somewhere to park: roughly the width of the opening again, running alongside it.

Plotted villa layouts provide that almost by accident. The compound wall runs past the gate, there is nothing built against it, and the approach is usually level. Sliding needs no clearance inside the driveway at all, which matters when the driveway is also where the car lives.

That is why, across Sarjapur Road, Whitefield and the newer layouts generally, sliding is what we fit most.

## When swing is still right

A swing gate suits a villa where the driveway is deep enough that the leaf's full arc stays clear with the car parked where it normally sits.

The test is worth doing yourself before anyone quotes: **park as usual, then open the gate by hand through its whole travel.** If the leaf comes anywhere near the car, a motor will not change that — it will just do it automatically, every day, and eventually someone will misjudge it.

On a double gate, each leaf is shorter and sweeps less, which is why wide villa gates are more often double than single.

## Slope is the condition that overrides everything

This is the one that catches people out, particularly toward the north and east of the city.

An arm motor assumes the leaf travels through a reasonably level arc. Where the driveway rises or falls through that arc, the arm has to fight the change in level and binds. The gate works for a while, then begins stalling at the same point, and the usual diagnosis — "the motor is weak" — is wrong. The motor is fine. The geometry is not.

The answer there is a **wheel-drive roller gate**: a swing gate carried by motorised wheels at the base of each leaf, rolling along the ground and following whatever the ground does. It needs a sound running surface under the arc, and it is not the cheapest option, but on a sloped plot it is the one that still works in year three.

We measure the fall across the arc rather than eyeballing it, because this decision is worth getting right first time. There is more in [roller gate vs sliding gate](/blog/roller-gate-vs-sliding-gate).

## Dust

Bengaluru dust is the quiet determinant of how a villa gate ages.

It settles into a sliding gate's track, and a gritty track makes the motor work measurably harder on every cycle. Nothing fails suddenly; the gate simply gets noisier and slower over a year or two until someone calls it worn out.

Two things fix this and both are cheap:

- **Sweep the track.** Five minutes, occasionally. It is the highest-value maintenance an owner can do.
- **Have the mechanism checked on a schedule.** A service visit cleans what sweeping does not reach — the rack engagement, the rollers, the channel.

On a layout still under construction, the first year is the worst for this, because building work generates grit continuously. The first service matters more there than anywhere.

## Monsoon

Less punishing here than on the coast, but two things are worth planning.

**Water at the motor.** Where the motor sits and how cable enters it decide whether a heavy spell is uneventful. This is an installation decision, not a product one.

**Power during it.** A DC motor carries battery backup on two 12V batteries, built in on some brands and a ₹5,500 add-on on others. An AC motor runs from your inverter connection, which we wire during installation if you ask. Every motor has a manual release key regardless — which, standing in rain with the car running, is the feature you will actually remember.

## If the villa is still being built

This is the cheapest moment to involve anyone, and most people miss it.

Before the gate is fabricated, we can tell the fabricator what frame stiffness, track and clearances the motor needs. Before first-fix, your electrician can run power to the gate position as part of the house wiring rather than as a trench across a finished driveway.

Both cost nothing at that stage. Both are awkward and visible later. We are happy to survey a plot that is still a shell, and it is the visit that saves the most money.
