Logistics runs around the clock and across locations. Branches, warehouses, and dispatch all depend on systems that stay connected — and when one location drops off the network, work stops there and delays ripple down the chain. Here's the complete IT checklist for a logistics or transport operation in Kerala.

## Step 1 — Map your locations and flows

Logistics IT is defined by geography. Before anything else, map every location — head office, branches, warehouses — and how information flows between them: where bookings come in, where dispatch happens, where accounting lives. This determines the connectivity and systems you need, and it's the step that's easiest to skip and most expensive to get wrong.

## Step 2 — Branch and warehouse hardware

Each location needs dependable workstations for booking, dispatch, and tracking, plus printers for documentation and a solid local network. Warehouses may need rugged hardware suited to the environment. Standardising across locations makes support far simpler. We supply and set up [hardware](/hardware) for every branch with GST invoicing.

## Step 3 — Reliable branch connectivity

This is the heart of logistics IT. Every location has to stay connected during operating hours, because a branch that goes dark stops dispatching. That means:

- A primary internet connection at each site, with **backup/failover** so one line dropping doesn't halt work.
- **Point-to-point wireless links** between nearby sites where running a line isn't practical.
- Secure connections between branches and head office.

Reliable, redundant connectivity is what keeps the operation moving. See our [networking](/networking) page for how we design this.

## Step 4 — Dispatch and accounting systems

Logistics runs on its operational software — dispatch, tracking, and accounting. We license and set up the tools your operation needs as part of our [software services](/software), configured so the workflow matches how you actually dispatch and bill.

## Step 5 — Integrations that cut manual work

The biggest hidden cost in logistics IT is re-entering the same information at every stage — booking, dispatch, tracking, invoicing — in separate systems. We connect those tools so data flows automatically instead of being keyed in again and again. Fewer handoffs mean fewer delays and fewer errors. This is our [automation](/automation) work.

## Step 6 — Always-on support

A failure at one point delays everything after it, so uptime is everything. An AMC scoped to your operating hours — including cover beyond a nine-to-five where your operation needs it — keeps hardware and networks maintained with priority response, so problems get handled before they cascade.

Every step serves one goal: no location goes dark, information flows without manual re-keying, and problems get handled before they ripple down the chain. In logistics, that reliability is the whole game.
