Few moments feel worse than a laptop that won't turn on with your only copy of something important on it — a thesis, a year of photos, the accounts. The good news is that "the laptop died" and "the data is gone" are two very different things, and far more often than people fear, the data is fine. Here's what's realistically recoverable, what isn't, and — crucially — what to do in the first hour to protect your chances.

## First, the most important point: a dead laptop usually has living data

Most of what people call a "dead laptop" is a failure that has nothing to do with where the files live:

- A cracked or black **screen**
- A dead **charging port** or battery
- A **motherboard** or power fault
- A laptop that **won't boot** into Windows or macOS

In all of these, the drive holding your files is usually untouched. We can very often recover everything simply by removing the drive and reading it in another machine. So before you panic: a laptop that won't start is not the same as lost data. Our guide on [why a laptop runs slow or won't behave](/blog/laptop-slow-fixes) covers the related "it's alive but struggling" cases.

## What's usually recoverable

- **Files on a working drive in a dead laptop** — very high success, often complete.
- **Deleted files** — often recoverable if you stop using the drive quickly.
- **A formatted or reset drive** — frequently recoverable, again if you stop immediately.
- **A drive with logical corruption** — often repairable enough to extract the data.

## What's harder — but not always hopeless

- **A physically failed drive** (clicking, not detected at all) — logical recovery may not work; this needs specialist clean-room equipment. Odds vary, and honesty matters here.
- **Severe water or impact damage that reached the drive** — depends entirely on whether the drive's internals survived.
- **SSDs that have failed at the controller level** — SSDs can be harder to recover than old spinning drives once the controller dies, because of how they store and encrypt data.

For these, we assess first and tell you plainly what the odds are before you spend anything, and we work with specialist labs for the genuinely hard cases.

## What to do in the first hour (and what not to do)

The biggest factor in recovery success is often what you do — or avoid doing — right after the failure:

**Do:**
- **Stop using the machine or drive** the moment you realise data may be at risk. For deleted or formatted files especially, every minute of continued use risks overwriting them.
- **Power it down** if it's struggling — repeated boot attempts stress failing hardware.
- **Bring it in as-is.** The sooner it's assessed, the better the odds.

**Don't:**
- **Don't keep rebooting** a laptop that won't start — it doesn't help and can make things worse.
- **Don't run random "recovery" tools** you found online on a failing drive — they can overwrite the very data you're trying to save.
- **Don't put a water-damaged laptop in rice and hope** — get it looked at; time matters more than folk remedies.

## The lesson everyone learns too late

Every data recovery job ends with the same thought: *I should have had a backup.* Recovery is a rescue, not a plan — sometimes it works, sometimes it doesn't, and it always costs more stress than prevention would have. Once your files are safe, set up automated backup so you never have to gamble on recovery again. ## The bottom line

A dead laptop usually doesn't mean dead data — and the fastest way to lose recoverable files is to keep using the machine. Stop, power down, and get it assessed.
