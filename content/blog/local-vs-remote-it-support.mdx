Remote IT support has real advantages: it's fast, it's often cheaper, and a good technician can fix a huge range of problems without leaving their desk. Any competent IT partner uses remote tools constantly. But there's a hard limit to what remote support can do — and for a business with physical premises, that limit is exactly where the expensive problems live. Here's why local still wins.

## What remote support does well

Let's be fair to remote first. A lot of IT work genuinely doesn't need anyone on-site:

- Software configuration and troubleshooting
- Email, licensing, and account setup
- Diagnosing many issues before deciding what to do
- Quick fixes and routine changes

For these, remote is faster than waiting for a visit, and a good partner reaches for it first. Speed is remote support's strength.

## Where remote support hits a wall

The problem is that a business isn't only software. When the failure is physical, remote can diagnose it but not fix it:

- **A failed drive or dead laptop** — someone has to physically replace it.
- **A dead router or switch** — needs hands on the hardware.
- **A cabling fault** — you can't re-run a cable over a video call.
- **Setting up a new office or branch** — the whole thing is physical.
- **A network that's down** — often the internet or the hardware is the problem, and no remote tool can reach a device that's offline.

These are also, not coincidentally, the failures that stop your business. And a remote-only provider's answer to them is "we'll arrange someone" — which means finding a local contractor anyway, on their timeline.

## The response-time difference

When something critical is down, the gap between local and remote-only is stark. A local partner can be on-site the same day. A remote-only or out-of-district provider is either flying someone in, sub-contracting locally, or telling you to wait. For a downed billing counter or a dead office network, same-day versus next-week is the difference between a bad morning and a lost week.

## The false economy of remote-only

Cheap remote-only outsourcing often looks attractive on price — until you read what it excludes. On-site work is usually not included, so the day hardware fails, you're paying a second, local provider anyway, at premium rates, with no relationship. Two providers, two bills, no accountability. A local partner who does both remote and on-site under one contract is frequently cheaper overall, and far simpler.

## The best of both

The point isn't that remote is bad — it's that remote *only* is incomplete. The strongest setup is a local partner who leads with remote tools for speed and shows up in person when the job needs hands on equipment. Fast fixes when they can be fast; a technician at your door when they can't. Under an [IT AMC](/amc), that's exactly how it works — remote-first response with on-site cover built in.

## The bottom line

For a business with real premises, hardware, and a network, remote-only support covers the easy half and leaves you exposed on the expensive half. A local partner covers both.
