Most small office networks grow by accident. A router goes in, then a few more devices, then CCTV, then a guest password shared with everyone — and nobody ever steps back to check whether the whole thing is secure. Usually it isn't. This is a practical, no-jargon checklist to fix that, without needing an enterprise budget.

## 1. Separate your networks

The single most important item. On most small office networks, everything sits together — staff PCs, guest phones, CCTV, accounting — so any compromised device can reach everything. Fix it by separating traffic:

- **Guest WiFi** on its own network, so visitors' devices never touch your business systems.
- **Business systems** (computers, shared files, accounting) on their own segment.
- **CCTV and IoT devices** isolated on yet another, since cheap connected devices are a common weak point.

This separation (using VLANs) means a problem in one area can't spread to the others. It's the highest-value change most offices can make.

## 2. Use a real firewall

The router your ISP provided is built to be cheap and get you online — not to protect a business. A proper business firewall gives you real control: it enforces the network separation above, filters traffic, and gives you visibility into what's happening. For anything beyond a couple of people, it's worth it. See our [networking](/networking) page for how we specify these.

## 3. Secure your WiFi properly

- Use **WPA2 or WPA3** encryption — never an open or WEP network.
- Set a **strong WiFi password**, and change it whenever someone with access leaves.
- Run a **separate guest network** so visitors never get your main password.
- **Rename the default network name** and change the router's default admin password — those defaults are public knowledge.

## 4. Lock down access

- Change every **default admin password** on routers, switches, and the firewall.
- Give staff **only the access their role needs**, not blanket access to everything.
- **Remove access promptly** when someone leaves — same day, not eventually.

## 5. Keep firmware and devices updated

Routers, switches, and firewalls run software that gets security fixes — and that almost nobody updates. Out-of-date network hardware is a common way in. Keep firmware current, and replace hardware that's too old to receive updates at all.

## 6. Protect the physical side too

Network security isn't only digital. A network cabinet or router sitting in the open, where anyone can plug in or reset it, is a gap. Keep network hardware in a secure spot, and don't leave live network ports accessible in public areas like a reception waiting room.

## 7. Back up, because security can still fail

No network is perfectly secure, so assume something will eventually get through and plan for it. Automated, off-site backups mean even a successful ransomware attack is recoverable. This overlaps with the basics in our [cybersecurity basics for small business](/blog/cybersecurity-basics-small-business-kerala) guide — network security and backups work together, not separately.

## Run through it honestly

Go down this list for your own office and mark what's actually in place. Most small offices find they're missing the first item — network separation — and often several others. None of it is expensive to fix; it's mostly setup and discipline.
