Sliding is the default gate in Bengaluru, and for a good reason: plotted layouts here are built with compound walls that run past the opening, which is exactly the space a sliding gate needs to park into.

Here is what motorising one costs, and what actually moves that number.

## The two tiers

Both figures include GST and cover the motor kit:

| Motor | From |
| --- | --- |
| Standard (AC) | ₹39,000 |
| Premium (DC) | ₹44,500 |

The kit is the motor sized to your gate, 2 remotes, a safety sensor pair, the rack, and the welding to fit it. Installation is quoted after the survey.

## The track decides more than the motor

This is the part most quotes skip and most problems come from.

A sliding gate rolls on a track. The motor drives a toothed rack fixed along the gate, but everything that motor does is transmitted through wheels running on that track. If the track is good, a correctly sized motor is almost silent and lasts years. If the track catches at one point, the motor will catch there too — with more force than you would apply, every single day.

**The test takes ten seconds.** Push the gate by hand, slowly, the whole way across. If it runs smoothly end to end, you are in good shape. If it drags, sticks or needs a shove at a particular spot, that is not something a motor fixes. It is something a motor fights.

Where we find that, we quote the track work separately from the automation, so you can see exactly what you are paying for rather than being handed one number with the repair hidden inside it.

## What else moves the installation figure

- **Where power is.** A point at the gate pillar is free to use. A run from the house is a trench.
- **The plinth.** The motor sits on one. If there is nowhere suitable, it has to be cast.
- **Gate weight.** Measured, not estimated from the width. Two gates of the same span can differ a lot once the infill is counted.
- **Frame stiffness.** A sliding gate is driven from one end, so a frame that flexes transmits the drive badly. Older gates sometimes need a stiffening member.

## AC or DC for a sliding gate

The gap is ₹5,500. What it buys:

**AC at ₹39,000** does the job. It starts and stops more abruptly than DC, which on a heavy gate you notice. It has no battery of its own, but it runs from your inverter connection during a power cut, which we wire during installation if you ask.

**DC at ₹44,500** ramps up and down rather than snapping on, runs quieter, takes battery backup on two 12V batteries, and is the only tier where voice control through Alexa or Google Home is possible on compatible setups.

For a house gate used a handful of times a day, AC is honest value. For a gate at a shared entrance cycling dozens of times daily, DC ages visibly better and the gap stops being a premium. We go through this in more detail in [AC vs DC gate motors](/blog/ac-vs-dc-gate-motor).

## Is a sliding gate noisy?

A correctly sized motor on a clean track is quiet enough that the gate rolling is the louder of the two. When a sliding gate is noisy, it is nearly always one of two things: a motor working against a gate heavier than it was sized for, or grit in the track.

Bengaluru dust is relentless about the second. A track that was clean in March will have collected enough by the time the year turns to make the motor work measurably harder. Sweeping it is five minutes and is the single most useful thing an owner can do between service visits.

## What to ask any quote

1. **AC or DC?** A quote that does not say is comparing nothing.
2. **Is the photocell pair included?** Without it you are buying a different, less safe system.
3. **Has anyone looked at the track?** A motor quoted without seeing the gate is a number, not a quote.
